Our trip up until this point has been mostly driven by seeing family, with extra sightseeing bits for us along the way. Now things change bit, the next week is an adventure just for us. We were going to travel the Llangollen canal in our own rented narrowboat.

Our first job was to get from Manchester Airport to Chirk, Wales. I chose to go via Transport for Wales (TfW) which connected in Chester. This is a trip across the industrial north of England, and the train station at Chester on a drizzly morning definitely kept to the industrial vibe.

![](https://images.kenchinpurcell.com/IDG_20260926_110109_992.jpg)

Chester is a mid-sized city, but Chirk is a small town near the Welsh border, although it is home to a huge factory that makes Cadbury hot chocolate mix. The marina where we were going to pick up our narrowboat was a mile and a half north of town, but our first job was to walk into town center from the train station, get some lunch and buy some supplies for the boat. We wound up eating at the Teddy Bear Cafe sandwich shop.

![](https://images.kenchinpurcell.com/IMG_3949.jpeg "Help")

I don't know if it is ubiquitous across the UK but Teddy Bear introduced us to "Coronation Chicken", a lightly curried chicken salad that was to be on most pub menus the coming week.

The helpful people at the Black Prince marina had told us that the boat's fridge was small, so we only bought a few things at the local Spar. Again, not sure for the whole of the UK but Chirk at least was replete with meat pies. I picked up a pork pie from the local butcher across the street from Teddy Bear.

Now we just needed to get ourselves, luggage, groceries and pork pie to the marina. The marina had earlier emailed me a list of small local taxi companies and strongly suggested pre-booking. Three weeks before the trip I dutifully called a couple of them up. One didn't pick up, the next did but then she just said "Love, call back closer to the date (click)". Hmph.

So while in Switzerland last week I called back and tried to arrange a ride. Accents were thick and Welsh taxi people seem very impatient, at least with Americans, but I _thought_ I got the job done.

But now here we were on the corner of Teddy Bear and Spar in Chirk and no taxi was showing up. Nervousness set in, we needed to be at the marina no later than 3:00 pm. It was already 2:30. The Coronation Chicken felt heavy in my stomach. I finally called they guy I thought I had booked for 2:00 pm, and he said in a miffed tone that he had been there at 12:00 pm and I wasn't (click). In theory we both speak English, but in practice...

So I tried the next entry on the list of taxi companies and my luck turned. The dispatcher and I seemed to understand each other, and she said that normally they don't have taxis near Chirk but one of theirs had just dropped off a fare near us, and just hold on a few minutes. And sure enough, a helpful and cheerful driver drove up a few minutes later. On the way to the marina he gave us some great advice on booking the return trip from the marina, _use the app_. Music to my ears.

Thorough tour of the boat, super brief driving lesson, and we were off.

Michele thought the canal starts out narrow, actually a nice wide section. Right into a one way tunnel, then the aqueduct. Pubs, highway. Found a quiet place to park, dinner on board.

Pretty much a yurt in a tube. Twelve-volt lifestyle. Diesel heater, hot water from the engine. Microwave and toaster when the engine is running, but didn’t need those. Stove on propane. Everything else twelve volt, pumps, toilet, lighting.
