We decided to spend a day off the water and stay on land. Our first adventure was the historic Llangollen Railway. The train running this fall Friday was a 1950's era motor coach. On summer weekends they bring out the steam engine.

![](https://images.kenchinpurcell.com/IDG_20261001_093910_578.jpg)

It seemed like a mostly volunteer operation, retirees in railway uniforms. The station and equipment looked great, well maintained.

![](https://images.kenchinpurcell.com/IDG_20261001_093940_422.jpg)

We motored our way up to Corwen, accompanied by an extremely mannered and slightly odd conductor, who would tell us fairly unremarkable things in a way that begged for a remarkable response. It was a little tiring. The scenery however was top notch as the railway followed River Dee up the valley.

Corwen is trying, but honestly it was also not that remarkable. Pretty, pleasant, worth a quick stroll.

![](https://images.kenchinpurcell.com/IDG_20261001_105006_174.jpg)

Then it was back on the train. There were two cars and we switched to the other one, but our strange conductor followed us. So we got told more unremarkable things on the way back.

![](https://images.kenchinpurcell.com/IDG_20261001_111904_276.jpg)

From many points in Llangollen you can see a ruin on a hill. After lunch we hiked up there.

![](https://images.kenchinpurcell.com/IDG_20261001_132043_379.jpg)

[Castell Dinas Brân](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Castell_Dinas_Brân) was built by Gruffydd Maelor II in the thirteenth century. (aside: "Gruffydd" is more or less pronounced "Griffith". See, Welsh isn't _that_ hard) It's an obvious spot for a castle, so there have been various fortifications on the hill, wood forts and so on, since 600 BC. Anyway, Gruffydd built an impressive stone castle in 1265, only for his sons to light the thing on fire 15 years later so it couldn't fall into English hands. Such was life in medieval Wales. It's been a ruin ever since. In Victorian times it was a popular tourist destination, with a small refreshment stand. Now it is a grassy scenic knoll.

![](https://images.kenchinpurcell.com/IMG_5640.jpeg)

That evening we went to dinner at the Corn Mill, a really tasty and friendly restaurant on the river. As we were enjoying our after meal peppermint tea...

![](https://images.kenchinpurcell.com/IMG_4014.jpeg)

Up walks Peter and Margaret, who were seated next to us. We had a friendly chat. They did a good job selling me on the narrowboat life, at least as an alternative to something like a motorhome.
