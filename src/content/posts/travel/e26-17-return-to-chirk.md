We ate breakfast in Llangollen at a fun cafe we had found, which that morning had three dogs that desperately wanted to check each other out while their owners sipped their tea. Then it was back on the boat and a fairly easy cruise back to Chirk. Our goal was to position the boat near the marina by evening so we could make a quick drop off the next morning.

Now once again I had the current behind me, so the engine was at an easy glide and I had the towpath on the right, which meant I wasn't in the brush when passing another boat. This was the easy direction. The canal looked scenic traveling eastward too.

![](https://images.kenchinpurcell.com/IMG_4016.jpeg)

After six days of navigating the narrowboat I was feeling pretty good about my steering. This morning I was threading the needle like a pro, no bumps or scrapes going under bridges or through the narrows. Yes-sir-ee, the boat was going right down the middle, no more wandering around. I was hot shit, an experienced narrowboat skipper. It was a lovely day with a refreshing breeze from the north and non-threatening clouds. 

The only tricky bit we had was trying not to run over several very novice canoeists coming the other way. There were a couple of us boats in a traffic jam trying to keep stopped and not drift into them as they bobbed and weaved around.

As we motored along I cooked up a plan for when we got to Trevor Basin, with the sharp right turn to go over the aqueduct. When we came the other way two days ago it wasn't quite perfect, there were some bumps. I could do better. In my mind I was going to emerge from under he bridge, elegantly bring to the boat to a full stop, swing the tiller over and do a beautiful 90 degree swivel to the right, assess if I had right of way on the aqueduct, and then proceed. 

So we got to that last bridge, squeezed under, and I was in Trevor Basin. To my immediate left was a raft of boats in the marina. Ahead was a concrete wall and a herd of tourists waiting to walk the bridge. To my right, the Pontcysyllte Aqueduct. I threw the boat into reverse, stopped... 

And then the stiff breeze from the aqueduct side promptly started pushing me sideways into the raft of boats. I swung the tiller over to no effect, I just kept going sideways. Tourists started to take interest. I had to wave off the boat behind us, they patiently stopped under the bridge and watched me flail around. Desperate, I purposely ran the nose of the boat into the concrete wall ahead of me, with a loud clunk. More heads turned. There's a bumper at the bow just for this situation, but it was still pretty clumsy. Now at least I had leverage and could turn the nose into the wind. After some more back-and-forths I finally got the boat pointed at the aqueduct.

My hot shit skipper phase was over.

It was super busy with pedestrians that morning on the aqueduct. Fortunately there were two volunteers at either end, with yellow vests on, directing foot and boat traffic. We got the go-ahead and proceeded across. I was still calming down from Trevor Basin, but then I was captured by the thrill of the aqueduct.

There was a crowd of Scandinavians on the other side, many with phones out videoing our progress. I briefly struck a pose.

Next was Froncysyllte again, with its adorable draw bridge.

{% youtube "[https://youtube.com/shorts/OGb0TmpreJA](https://youtu.be/b7xkV87GHEQ)" %}

We moored in the correct spot this time for Froncysyllte (_downstream_ of the bridge) and had lunch again at the Aqueduct Inn. For all the crowds down at the aqueduct the Inn was fairly quiet. The owner this time talked about Americans buying up English football teams, like Tom Brady's part ownership of Birmingham City. It must be a very mixed bag for these fans of tier 2 teams. They are proud of their team and probably aren't excited about outside ownership, but the teams need the money to compete.

![](https://images.kenchinpurcell.com/IDG_20261002_164945_541.jpg)

That afternoon we chose a quiet spot on the canal bank for the night, just upstream of the marina. An hour later Balto tied up next to us. We chatted a bit; he's a navy guy from Portsmouth. I said I'm not navy but I'm from a navy town too. We both had a good week.
