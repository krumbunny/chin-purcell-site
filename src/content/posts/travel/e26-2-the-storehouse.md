The [V&A](https://www.vam.ac.uk/), Britain’s museum of decorative arts, is one of my favorite places. The main museum is near Hyde park, and the main floor has loads of popular exhibits, like the armor collection. My usual route though is straight upstairs to the quiet 4th floor, down the corridors of furniture and into the studio pottery area. They have a treasure trove of English and Japanese pots, plus fascinating exhibits like a reconstruction of Lucy Rie’s postwar London studio. 

Like any big museum the V&A has far more objects than it can exhibit, so when I read last year they had built a new storehouse that was open to the public I knew that I wanted to go. Even better though, I saw on their website that the Storehouse has a program called [Order an Object](https://www.vam.ac.uk/info/order-an-object), where visitors can request five objects from the online catalog and make an appointment to see and handle them up close. 

For a potter this is irresistible. The way you can tell a potter in a ceramics shop is that they’re the one picking up the pots, turning them over and running their hands along the sides. 

I decided I wanted to see some old pots with dark iron saturate glazes. I found two Song Dynasty (about 1000 years old) pots, a Japanese “oil spot” pot from about 120 years ago, and a more contemporary Bernard Leach pot for comparison. I had one object to go, thought about what Michele might be interested in, and had a brainwave. It turns out they have a 200 year old Shishi gashira (Japanese lion dance costume), in the same tradition as Michele’s Shishi Mai group.
